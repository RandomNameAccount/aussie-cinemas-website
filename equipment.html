Projector history at a glance

Broad periods only. Dates vary from place to place and will be documented with sources.

  1. 1890s–1920sSilent era

    Hand-cranked and then motor-driven projectors, often in temporary halls.

  2. Late 1920s–1930sSound arrives

    Sound-on-disc and then optical sound transform projection boxes.

  3. 1950sWidescreen and drive-ins

    Wider screens, new lenses and the spread of drive-ins.

  4. 1960s–1980sAutomation

    Long-play platters let one projectionist run several screens.

  5. 2000s–2010sDigital conversion

    Digital projectors replace most 35mm equipment.

Film formats

Gauges drawn to their relative widths.

35mm

The standard theatrical gauge for most of the 20th century.

70mm

Wide gauge for premium roadshow presentations.

16mm

Smaller gauge for halls, schools and travelling shows.

Super 8

Home-movie gauge, sometimes used in small venues.

IMAX 15/70

70mm film running horizontally, 15 perforations per frame.
